All principal logos live in /photos/partners/ — NOT in /photos/
directly. They appear as a static wall on the homepage and on the
/partners/ page. Filenames match the brand, for example:
  gilbarco.svg   raasm.png   custom.png   itl.png

Most are .webp, a few are .png. Any <img> must use the exact
extension. To replace one, keep the same filename AND extension,
otherwise the slot falls back to showing the brand name as text.

Lafon, PIUSI, TransTank and Transponder Technologies were dropped from
every wall at the business's request. Their logo files (lafon.webp,
piusi.svg, transtank.webp, tt.webp) have been deleted. Orpak and Invenco
were added to the homepage and /partners/ walls, so those lists now hold
19 brands (orpak.jpg and invenco.jpg were already in the folder).

Cebora was dropped from both walls at the business's request, and its
logo file (cebora.png) has been deleted.

The walls are no longer a grid of equal boxes. Each logo carries a tier
class that sets how large it is drawn — .t1 for the biggest principals,
.t2 for the rest — so the wall shows which relationships are the largest.
Sizing is height-driven: the tier fixes the logo's height and its width
follows its own shape, so the marks sit at natural, varied widths rather
than in equal boxes. The walls are also BANDED: each tier is wrapped in a
.tier row-group so a whole row is one size and a tier never shares a row
with another. To promote or demote a brand, move its cell into the other
tier's .tier group and change its class. Entries are listed by tier and
then alphabetically within it. (A third size, .t3, still exists in the
CSS for the customer walls; the principals wall stopped using it — the
list is short enough that two sizes read more cleanly.)

Current principals tiering (homepage + /partners/):
  t1  Airtec, Custom, Gilbarco Veeder-Root, HPA-Faip, ITL Technologies,
      RAASM, Red Jacket, TCS
  t2  Brain Bee, DOMS, Durapipe, Gasboy, Honeywell, Invenco, OPW,
      Orpak, Postec, Wincor Nixdorf
The Hanindo Citra page shows its own petroleum-relevant subset of these,
tiered the same way.

orpak.jpg and invenco.jpg were shipped with wide blank margins baked into
the canvas (Orpak filled only 43% of its height, Invenco 67%), so at a
fixed row height their marks rendered far smaller than neighbours like
Gasboy and Postec. Both files were trimmed to their content bounding box,
so they now fill the row height like every other logo. Trimming Invenco
also removed the faint off-white (#f7f7f7) margin that used to show as a
box on the white band. If either is ever replaced, supply a tightly
cropped file (no surrounding whitespace) so it renders at full size.

PROJECT PHOTOS  (/automotive/projects.html)  ->  photos/automotive/projects/
Unlike every other slot on this list, these have no fixed filenames: the
page grows a new entry per project rather than filling a fixed layout.

Each project is a SET of photos in one folder, named after the project
with a number on the end:

  <project>-1.jpg   the lead photo, the only one shown on the page
  <project>-2.jpg   the rest, in the order to step through them
  <project>-3.jpg   ...

So a project filed as "dealer-workshop-jakarta" gives
dealer-workshop-jakarta-1.jpg, -2.jpg, -3.jpg and so on. Landscape, at
least ~1200px wide; they open full screen, so bigger is better here than
on a card. Any number of photos per project — one photo is fine, it just
shows without the stack behind it.

Alongside the photos each project needs one line of scope ("lifts, tyre
service and lubrication"), and a sector plus region ("dealer workshop,
Jabodetabek"). A client NAME only where that customer has cleared being
named publicly; otherwise the project runs anonymous and the sector and
region carry it.

Sizing note for any future lineup. The card's picture box gives 329x162
at desktop, a ratio of 2.03. A single portrait shot is height-limited and
leaves most of the width empty — which is why the Tyre Service card, on
one C880, looked so sparse. Three machines side by side come out around
2.3:1, so the image is width-limited instead and the machines draw about
138px against the 162px a single product gets. That is the trade for
showing three, and it is the right trade on a card whose own caption
promises three. Do not pad the canvas to force 2.03; padding does not
make the machines any bigger.
